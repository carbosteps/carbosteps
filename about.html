Our story

Born on the French Riviera.

It began in the south of France with one man determined to engineer his way out of his foot pain.

01 — The origin

Born from pain.

On the French Riviera, Franck's days began early: pruning, planting, and tending gardens beneath the Mediterranean sun. In the evenings, he returned to his other world. An artist, designer, and instinctive inventor, he transformed ideas into artworks and functional objects.

The pain had been building since 2019. At first, it was easy to dismiss: a foot ache in the morning that diminished during the day. But gradually, ignoring it was no longer an option. In 2022, a doctor finally gave it a name: a heel spur.

What followed will sound familiar to anyone who has lived with persistent heel pain. Round after round of shockwave therapy, none of it lasting. Franck tried one off-the-shelf insole after another. Most did nothing. The few that helped lost their effectiveness as soon as the material gave out.

Working on his feet from sunrise to sundown, Franck wore through everything faster than it was designed to last.

Every solution had the same weakness: relief came with an expiry date.

“Foam absorbs energy and loses it. Carbon fiber stores it and gives it back.”

Franck, Co-founder & Designer

Franck started looking for a material that could provide real support — and keep providing it.

Conventional foam gradually compresses under repeated load, while plastic can lose its strength and shape over time. Carbon fiber behaves differently. Used in aerospace and motorsport for its combination of strength, flexibility, and resilience, it can carry weight, flex under pressure, and return to its original form.

So Franck began building his own solution. Sketches drawn at his kitchen table became handmade prototypes. With input from a physiotherapist, he mapped where the foot needed support and how that support should respond.

Three years of experimentation led to the Carbon Motion System: a two-blade carbon fiber mechanism calibrated to the wearer's weight. It provides firm support beneath the heel and arch while flexing with each step. Like a discreet spring beneath the foot, it compresses under load and returns as the foot rolls forward — supporting the movement without feeling rigid or intrusive.

For Franck, the difference was lasting. The pain that had shaped his mornings for years finally receded — and stayed away.

02 — The meeting

Rethinking comfort.

In July 2025, Bruno and Franck met in Antibes.

Bruno was skeptical at first. The carbon fiber insole looked hard, even rigid — the opposite of everything he associated with comfort.

Then he tried it.

The hardness he expected was not there. Instead, he felt a gentle spring effect — noticeable at first, then so natural that he soon forgot he was wearing it.

By the end of the day, Bruno noticed something he had not been looking for: his back felt lighter. The change was subtle, but unmistakable.

Franck had not simply made a better insole. He had challenged the premise that comfort must come from softness. CarboSteps creates comfort through controlled elasticity — support that gives and returns, rather than cushioning alone.

Franck — Co-founder & Designer

Franck has invented all his life, alongside whatever job paid the bills — shops, bakeries, restaurants, gardens. Years of shaping resin by hand are why he could build a carbon fiber sole; his own pain is why he did.

Bruno — Co-founder & CEO

A career in Côte d'Azur real estate — luxury and commercial, first as an agent, then buying and restructuring properties of his own. Bruno backs CarboSteps and builds the company around the invention: capital, marketing, operations.

03 — The venture

Invented in France. Built for America.

Interest was not the same as conviction. Bruno and Franck were not looking for encouragement; they were looking for scrutiny. They put the prototype in front of professionals and invited them to challenge it.

Their feedback helped define the boundary CarboSteps still respects today. It is not a medical treatment and does not claim to be one. Its action is mechanical: firm support beneath the heel and arch, combined with a system that flexes and returns as the foot moves through each step.

They filed a US patent application — not simply for an insole, but for the architecture behind it. The Carbon Motion System is designed to work in two ways: placed inside the shoes people already own, or integrated directly into footwear engineered around the system.

Launching in the United States was a deliberate choice. It is a market that adopts new technology quickly, and one where an independent brand can reach its buyers directly. CarboSteps LLC was incorporated in New Mexico, and the work of bringing the system to market began.

France is where CarboSteps was invented. America is where Bruno and Franck chose to prove it.

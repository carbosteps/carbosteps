How it works

A spring, not a cushion.

Every step asks your foot for a certain amount of work. When that demand stays above what the foot can absorb, the ache sets in — thousands of times a day. CarboSteps doesn't numb that. It takes a share of the load.

Carbon Motion System · side profile · two blades, heel to arch · patent pending

Two blades, one gap.

  • 01

    It compresses under load. Your weight closes the gap between the blades. The carbon bends — it does not squash — and stores that energy instead of swallowing it.

  • 02

    It gives the energy back. As you roll onto the front of your foot, the blade returns to shape and releases what it stored, at the moment you're pushing off.

  • 03

    It stops before your toes. Three-quarter length: it ends behind the ball of your foot, so your forefoot moves exactly as it always has.

  • 04

    The rear is rounded. The mechanism stays engaged whether you land square, slightly inside or slightly outside.

01 — Heel strike

The impact lands here first.

The blade takes the shock at the heel and starts to bend, so less of it travels straight up through you.

02 — Mid stance

The gap closes.

Your full weight passes over the arch. The two blades come together and hold the energy rather than losing it.

03 — Push off

It comes back.

The blade returns to shape and releases what it stored, forward, into the next step. That is the part foam cannot do.

Made for your weight

Your CarboSteps will be calibrated to you.

A spring only works if it's tuned to what it carries. This is the part almost nobody does. A blade stiff enough for a 220-pound frame barely moves under a 130-pound one — it becomes a plank. Too soft, and it bottoms out and does nothing at all.

So CarboSteps isn't made in one stiffness. You're matched to a blade calibrated to your weight, which is why it can be firm enough to genuinely support you and still disappear under your foot.

Before you decide

The questions people actually ask.

Will they fit the shoes I already have?

That's what the three-quarter length is for. CarboSteps covers the heel and the arch and stops behind the ball of your foot, and the carbon is thin — far thinner than a moulded support. It's designed to go into your existing shoes rather than to send you shopping for new ones.

Very tight dress shoes and anything with almost no depth are the exception, as they are for any insole.

I've tried insoles before and they stopped working. Why is this different?

Because of what gives out. Foam and plastic relieve by compressing, and compression is exactly what they lose — they pack down and the relief fades with them. Carbon doesn't work by compressing; it works by bending and returning to shape, which is what it is made to do repeatedly.

That's also why there's a two-year guarantee on the product and three months for you to test the claim yourself.

How do you know which stiffness I need?

From your weight. A blade is a spring, and a spring is specified by the load it carries — that single number does most of the work. You enter it at checkout along with your shoe size, and you're matched to the corresponding blade.

Do I have to break them in?

There's no prescribed routine. They're firm, but the flex is tuned to your weight rather than fixed, which is what stops them feeling like a board underfoot. Most people put them in and carry on; if you want to ease into them over a few days, that's fine too.

Is this a medical device?

No, and we're careful about that. CarboSteps is registered as an FDA Class I exempt device (21 CFR 890.3475). It relieves fatigue and absorbs impact. It is not a treatment, we don't claim it treats any specific condition, and it isn't a substitute for seeing a professional about a problem that concerns you.

What if they simply don't work for me?

Send them back within three months and we refund what you paid. That's the whole point of the three months — it's long enough to include the ordinary days, the long ones, and the travel, rather than a week where nothing much happens.
